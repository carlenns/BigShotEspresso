# Prior Art and Existing Knowledge

> **Status:** Search framework opened 2026-08-29; substantive search not yet completed  
> **Rule:** Do not begin with the conclusion that BigShotEspresso is novel. Record relevant and adverse findings honestly.

## Purpose

This register documents established techniques and relevant products, literature, patents, standards, and technical knowledge before or during experiments. It supports experiment design and an honest assessment of whether a technological uncertainty remains.

This is not a patentability, freedom-to-operate, legal, or SR&ED opinion.

## Required source record

For every material source, record:

1. Full title, author/organization, publication date, and stable link/identifier.
2. Date accessed and, where permitted, an archived copy or bibliographic record.
3. Source type and credibility limitations.
4. Problem solved.
5. Method used.
6. Data and evaluation conditions.
7. What the evidence demonstrates.
8. What it does not demonstrate.
9. Relevance to a specific BigShot experiment or uncertainty.
10. Whether it changes, narrows, or resolves the proposed research question.

## Search areas

- Espresso logging and recommendation applications

- Recommendation systems and sparse-feedback methods
- Time-series, concept drift, and non-stationary systems
- Confidence, uncertainty, calibration, and abstention/selective prediction
- Personalization and cold-start approaches
- Sequential decision methods
- Relevant patents and published applications

## Search protocol

For each formal experiment:

1. Define the specific uncertainty and terms before searching.
2. Search multiple source classes, including adverse terminology and known commercial products.
3. Prefer primary technical sources for method claims.
4. Record search strings, databases, dates, filters, and exclusions.
5. Read the actual source; do not rely on search snippets or AI summaries.
6. Record contradictory evidence and solutions that may already solve the problem.
7. Update the experiment's baseline or stop the experiment if existing knowledge resolves the uncertainty.
